## How PKNIC and .pk Registration Actually Work

If you want a **.pk domain** — that means `yourbrand.pk`, `yourbrand.com.pk`, `.net.pk` or the other local extensions — you are dealing with **PKNIC**, Pakistan's official registry for the country-code top-level domain.

PKNIC is the authoritative body. It does not usually sell domains directly to the public; instead it accredits a network of **registrars and resellers** who handle the sales, paperwork and support. That means your .pk purchase almost always goes through a local company, even though the registry behind it is PKNIC.

The part that surprises most first-time buyers is the **verification requirement**. .pk registration is not a pure walk-up-and-pay purchase like a .com. Registrants typically need to:

- Provide a **CNIC (for individuals) or NTN plus registration documents (for companies)**.
- Submit a signed registration form or complete an online verification step through the reseller.
- Wait for the reseller to submit the application to PKNIC, which usually takes anywhere from a few hours to a few days.

This paperwork exists for good reason: it ties the domain to a real entity in Pakistan, which makes .pk domains harder to abuse and gives local businesses a registrable namespace. But it also means you cannot click "buy now" and have a .pk live in five minutes the way a .com works.

One more practical note: because the registry is PKNIC and not a US-based corporation, disputes and renewals are governed by **local policies and Pakistani law**. If a problem ever arises, you deal with the Pakistani ecosystem, not an ICANN helpline in a different time zone.

## How to Pay for a Domain from Pakistan

The payment question is usually the real blocker, not the choice of registrar. Here is how the practical payment landscape looks in 2026.

**PayPal and international cards.** The big international registrars — Namecheap, GoDaddy, Porkbun — accept PayPal and major credit/debit cards. If you have a PayPal account linked to a bank account or card that can fund it, you can buy any of them from Pakistan. This is the cleanest route and the one most of our readers end up using.

**Cards that work.** Many Pakistani debit and prepaid cards now work for international online purchases, but not all of them do. The card needs international e-commerce enabled, and some banks block foreign transactions by default. If your card is declined, call your bank and ask for international online payments to be switched on before you blame the registrar.

**Local resellers and wallets.** Here is where the Pakistan-specific reality kicks in. Many local hosting and domain resellers — companies you will find by searching for "buy domain in Pakistan" — accept **Easypaisa, JazzCash, bank transfer and cash deposit**. That makes them the only realistic option if you have no PayPal and no usable international card. The trade-off is that you are buying from a reseller, so you should read the red-flags section below carefully.

**Do not pay cash in person for a domain.** Some sellers offer cash-on-delivery for domains and hosting. It works, but you get no receipt trail and no leverage if the domain never lands in your account. Prefer a tracked payment method.

Whichever way you pay, the principle is identical: the domain must be **registered in your name, in your control panel**, and you must be able to see it. Payment is the start, not the end.

## .com vs .pk: A Practical Decision Framework

Stop thinking "which is better" and start thinking "what is this domain for." The right answer changes with the business.

- **Choose .com when** your audience is national or international, you want the widest recognition, or you plan to market online (ads, email, social) where .com is the assumed default. Almost every well-known Pakistani brand people type from memory uses .com.
- **Choose .pk when** your .com is already taken, you want to signal "official local business" to Pakistani customers, or the extra local trust is worth the verification friction and typically higher price.
- **Choose .com even when your business is purely local** — you can still rank for "in Lahore" with a .com. Search engines localise content, not just extensions.
- **Buy both if the brand matters to you.** Many businesses register the .com as the primary domain and the .pk defensively so nobody else can park on their brand. The .pk can redirect to the .com.

The single most honest rule: **if the .com is available at a normal price, take it.** If a squatter wants an absurd amount for it, evaluate your alternatives — a slightly different .com, or the .pk — rather than paying a random stranger thousands of rupees for a name they never used.

## WHOIS Privacy: What It Is and Whether You Need It

Every domain has a public registry record called **WHOIS** that traditionally displays the registrant's name, email, address and phone number. Without protection, your personal details are publicly searchable — and spam bots harvest them within days.

WHOIS privacy replaces your real contact details in the public record with the registrar's forwarding address, while you still legally own the domain. Your genuine details stay in your account for when authorities or the registrar need them.

For Pakistan specifically, privacy matters because your CNIC-related data is sensitive. A .com domain from an international registrar will almost always let you add privacy for **$0–10/year** (Porkbun and Cloudflare include it free; Namecheap includes it too). This is worth switching on even though it costs money on some registrars.

The catch with **.pk domains**: privacy availability depends on the reseller and on PKNIC's policies for the specific extension, and it is not always offered. If your .pk reseller cannot provide WHOIS privacy, at least use a dedicated business email and a P.O. box rather than your home address on the record.

## Ownership and Transfer Rules Under ICANN

Two ICANN-backed rules matter more than anything else you will read in a domain article, because they are the difference between owning your domain and merely renting it from a seller.

**First: the domain is registered in the buyer's name.** Under ICANN's transfer policy, the registrant listed on the account owns the domain. A reseller who registers domains under their own name and "gives you access" is a legal problem waiting to happen. Insist on the domain living in an account you control.

**Second: you can transfer after 60 days.** ICANN guarantees that a domain can be moved to a different registrar **free of charge after 60 days** from registration or the last transfer. You unlock the domain with a code at your current registrar, and the new registrar pulls it over. Renewals carry over, so you are not paying twice. This is your protection against a bad registrar — if one raises prices, you leave.

Do not be afraid to transfer a domain. The process takes a few days and the receiving registrar will walk you through it. It is the single most effective way to keep your renewal costs honest.

## How to Register Your Domain: Step by Step

Here is the exact process, whether you buy a .com or a .pk.

1. **Check availability first.** Search your preferred name across .com and .pk before you commit to anything. If the .com is taken, check common variations like adding your city or industry word before giving up on the name.
2. **Choose the registrar.** For .com, pick from the table above based on your priorities (Porkbun for price, Cloudflare for long-term cost, Namecheap for balance). For .pk, shortlist PKNIC-accredited resellers with good reviews and clear pricing.
3. **Set up your account.** Use a real, monitored email address — domain expiry and transfer notices go there, and this email is how you prove ownership.
4. **Add the domain to the cart and skip the add-ons.** Say no to upsells like "domain insurance," premium support and website builder subscriptions you do not need. Enable WHOIS privacy if it costs extra and you want it.
5. **Pay with PayPal or a working international card.** If both fail, fall back to a reputable local reseller that accepts Easypaisa or JazzCash — but verify the red flags below first.
6. **Complete verification.** For .com, this is usually just a confirmation email. For .pk, expect to submit your CNIC or company documents through the reseller and wait for PKNIC processing.
7. **Confirm it is in your account.** Log in, find the domain under "My Domains," and check that your name is the registrant. Set **auto-renewal on** and note the renewal date in your calendar.
8. **Point it at your website.** Change the nameservers to your hosting provider's, or set the DNS records yourself. Allow up to 48 hours for DNS changes to spread globally.

That is the whole job. From "I want a website" to "the domain is live" takes under an hour for a .com and a few days for a .pk.

## Red Flags to Avoid When Buying a Domain

Not every seller in the Pakistani market plays fair. These are the warning signs to walk away from:

- **The domain is not in your name.** The biggest red flag of all. If a reseller insists the domain stays under their account "for support purposes," walk away. Your name must be the registrant.
- **You "own" the domain but cannot see it.** No control panel access, no login, "we manage it for you" — that is not ownership, that is dependency.
- **Prices that are wildly below market.** A .com at "Rs 500 one-time, no renewal" is not a .com. Suspiciously cheap deals usually mean stolen, revoked or resold-after-the-fact domains.
- **Pressure to pay in advance for years of "service."** Legitimate renewals are yearly and visible on the registry. Anyone demanding multi-year prepayment with no receipt is building a trap.
- **No written invoice or receipt.** Real resellers issue proper invoices. If the only proof of purchase is a JazzCash transaction ID, treat it with suspicion.
- **Registrar names that sound like a well-known brand.** Some resellers name themselves to look like GoDaddy or Namecheap. Check the real registrar on the WHOIS record, not the website's branding.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I Need PKNIC Accreditation to Buy a .pk Domain?

No. PKNIC accreditation is required to **sell** .pk domains as a registrar or reseller, not to buy one. Any individual or company can register a .pk domain by going through an accredited registrar or reseller. What you do need is the verification documents — a CNIC for individuals, or NTN and registration papers for companies — that the reseller will ask for before submitting your application.

### Can I Pay for a Domain with Easypaisa or JazzCash?

Yes, but only through **local resellers** — international registrars like Namecheap and Porkbun do not accept Easypaisa or JazzCash directly. If you have PayPal or a working international card, the global route is easiest. If you are paying by wallet, you will be buying through a Pakistani reseller, so confirm the domain is registered in your name before you transfer the money.

### Is .com or .pk Better for a Pakistani Business?

For most businesses, **.com is the better default**: it is cheaper, instant to register, and the extension people type automatically, whether your customers are in Karachi or Canada. Choose .pk when the .com you want is taken or when the local "official Pakistani business" signal is worth the extra verification and cost. The strongest setup for a brand that matters is .com as primary with .pk registered defensively and redirected.

### Can I Transfer My .com to a Cheaper Registrar?

Yes. ICANN rules guarantee that after **60 days** from registration or the last transfer, you can move your domain to any registrar free of charge. Unlock the domain at your current registrar, get the transfer code, and the new registrar handles the rest — your remaining renewal time carries over. If you have been paying a premium at one registrar, this is the standard, safe way to fix it.

### What Is WHOIS Privacy and Do I Need It?

WHOIS privacy hides your real name, address and phone number from the public domain record, replacing them with the registrar's forwarding details. You still own the domain and remain reachable. For Pakistani registrants it is especially worth it because the public record can expose CNIC-linked data. Porkbun, Namecheap and Cloudflare include it free on .com; with .pk domains, availability depends on the reseller.
