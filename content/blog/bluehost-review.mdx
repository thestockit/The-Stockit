For someone who has never touched a hosting account before, Bluehost is arguably the easiest onboarding in the business — which is precisely why its WordPress-first identity resonates with beginners rather than with experienced developers.

The **Basic** plan is the one behind Bluehost's famous $2.95/mo headline (on longer terms). It includes one website, 10 GB of SSD storage, free SSL and a free domain for the first year. That 10 GB is the tightest limit in the lineup — it disappears surprisingly quickly once you add plugins, uploads and a media library — so treat Basic as a starting plan, not a long-term home for a growing site.

**Choice Plus** is the plan Bluehost pushes hardest, and for most people it is the one to buy: unlimited websites, domain privacy included, and automated backups that Basic lacks. **Online Store** swaps in WooCommerce-specific features, store themes and payment integrations for people selling products. **Pro** adds a dedicated IP, higher resource ceilings and a larger visitor allowance — the closest Bluehost's shared tier gets to a performance plan.

Every plan includes unlimited bandwidth, free SSL certificates, a free domain for the first year, and the 30-day money-back guarantee. Note the pattern across all four: the longer your initial term, the lower the monthly promo price — and the larger the bill you are locked into when renewal arrives.

Read those numbers honestly: Bluehost sits mid-pack. A typical Time to First Byte in the 450–550 ms range is perfectly usable, and most WordPress sites on Bluehost will load acceptably once you add a caching plugin — but it is not in the same league as Hostinger's sub-250 ms performance or Scala's roughly 280 ms. Bluehost's shared infrastructure is reliable and well-maintained; it is simply not the fastest architecture, and Bluehost has never pretended otherwise.

Uptime, to its credit, has been good. Across our monitoring windows Bluehost has stayed comfortably above the 99.9% its guarantee promises. The speed story is straightforward: fine for the vast majority of WordPress sites, frustrating if your business depends on squeezing every last millisecond out of a page load, and a non-starter if you are chasing perfect Core Web Vitals scores from day one.

Two caveats worth stating. First, on the cheaper plans the limits — Basic's 10 GB of storage and single-site allowance — tend to bite before speed does. Second, Bluehost's value only shows when you combine its performance with its ease of use; a fast site you cannot manage is worse than a mid-speed site you actually run. For most people, Bluehost's trade-off is the right one.

## The "Control Panel": CPanel Under the Hood

Bluehost's own marketing talks about its "Control Panel," but experienced users will recognise it immediately: it is a customised, enhanced version of cPanel, the industry-standard control panel. That is good news for anyone coming from another host, and a shortcut for beginners, because every tutorial, forum answer and video walkthrough about cPanel applies directly to Bluehost.

The home screen is a polished dashboard with quick links to your WordPress install, email, domains and security tools. Click deeper and you land in familiar cPanel territory: the file manager, MySQL databases, cron jobs, subdomains, and the email section for creating accounts and forwarders. If you have used cPanel before, you will feel at home within minutes. If you have not, the sheer number of options can be intimidating — but Bluehost's WordPress-first design means you rarely need to touch most of it.

Compared with modern custom panels like Hostinger's hPanel or Scala's SPanel, Bluehost's panel feels a little dated and loads a bit slower. But familiarity is a feature. For WordPress users specifically, the panel-to-WordPress integration — auto-login, managed updates, and staging on higher plans — is better than any host that merely layers stock cPanel on top of shared servers.

## Support: 24/7 and Genuinely Helpful (Most of the Time)

Bluehost runs the classic US-host trifecta: 24/7 live chat, phone support, and a ticket system. That alone puts it ahead of the chat-only budget hosts. In our experience, live chat is the best channel — median wait times in the low single-digit minutes, with agents who can actually change settings on your account rather than reading from a script.

Phone support is the real differentiator. When you are stuck and the chat queue is long, being able to call a human is genuinely valuable, and it is something Hostinger and Scala do not offer at all. That said, phone queues can stretch during peak hours, and the depth of answers varies from agent to agent. The worst interactions we have had with Bluehost support were not wrong answers but slow ones — and for most users, the reassurance that a person is reachable outweighs that.

One honest criticism: Bluehost's support is trained to sell. Conversations occasionally steer toward paid add-ons — SiteLock, CodeGuard, upgrade pitches — even when your issue is minor. It is persistent but rarely aggressive, and you can decline without friction.

## Security: Free SSL and the Add-On Puzzle

Security at Bluehost starts with the essentials that ship on every plan: free SSL certificates (auto-renewed and applied across your domains), automated backups on Choice Plus and above, and routine server hardening. That baseline is genuinely fine for most sites — it is the same baseline most reputable hosts provide, and it covers the majority of beginners' real-world exposure.

Then comes the add-on puzzle. Bluehost sells **SiteLock** for malware scanning and removal, **CodeGuard** for automated off-site backups, and domain privacy (included free on Choice Plus and above). Here is the honest take: the free baseline is enough for most small sites, and you can cover most of the gap with a good security plugin plus the backups your plan already includes. We would only pay for CodeGuard if you run a growing site where losing a day of changes would actually hurt, and we would generally skip SiteLock unless you specifically want continuous scanning managed by the host.

## Renewal Pricing: the Reality Check

This is the section that decides whether Bluehost is actually a good deal, and the honest answer is that the intro price is not the real price. Bluehost's promotional rates — around $2.95–$13.95/mo depending on plan and term — apply to your initial term only. When that term ends, the plan renews at the standard rate, and for Bluehost those jumps are among the steepest in the industry.

Expect renewal prices roughly **double to triple the promo rate**: Basic renewing around $12/mo, Choice Plus around $22/mo, and Pro well above $30/mo. The gap is built into the business model — capture with a low first-year price, monetize with renewal margin. Every budget host does some version of this (Hostinger renews at roughly triple its intro rate too), but Bluehost's higher absolute numbers make the jump harder to absorb.

Practical advice: budget for the renewal price, not the promo price. If $12/mo for Basic is more than you want to pay forever, either pick a cheaper host or treat Bluehost as a two-to-three-year plan and move when the term ends. The 30-day money-back guarantee means you can try it with almost no risk — but it does nothing to protect you from the year-two invoice.

## Who Is Bluehost Actually For?

Bluehost is for people who want WordPress to work without a steep learning curve. If that describes you — a blogger, a small business owner, a freelancer launching your first real site — Bluehost is one of the easiest recommendations we make. The onboarding is fast, the support is reachable, the panel is familiar, and the WordPress.org endorsement removes the "which host should I pick?" anxiety entirely.

Bluehost is not for you if raw speed is your priority, if you are on a tight budget beyond year one, or if you are launching something with serious, predictable traffic from day one. Speed-obsessed buyers and heavy-traffic sites should look at Hostinger, LiteSpeed-based hosts, or managed WordPress hosts with higher performance ceilings. If you outgrow shared hosting quickly, Bluehost's VPS and dedicated tiers exist, but you will likely find better value elsewhere at that level.

## Bluehost vs the Closest Competitors

The two hosts that come up most often against Bluehost are Hostinger and SiteGround, and each comparison is lopsided in a different direction.

**Bluehost vs Hostinger.** Hostinger is faster (roughly half Bluehost's TTFB in our tests), cheaper at both intro and renewal, and ships NVMe storage and LiteSpeed on every plan. What Bluehost gives you back is the WordPress.org recommendation, phone support, and a more beginner-oriented onboarding experience. If budget and speed are your priorities, Hostinger wins this one; if you want the industry's most trusted WordPress name and a phone number to call at 3am, Bluehost does.

**Bluehost vs SiteGround.** SiteGround posts better performance and, in our experience, more consistently excellent support than Bluehost — but it is pricier, renews high as well, and its storage caps are tighter. SiteGround also lacks the WordPress.org endorsement. For someone who wants premium service and does not mind paying for it, SiteGround edges ahead; for a true beginner on a budget, Bluehost's combination of price and simplicity is hard to beat.

The honest bottom line on all three: they are all good hosts, and the best one for you depends on which weakness you can tolerate, not on which one is "the best." ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Bluehost Good for WordPress?

Yes. Bluehost is one of the few hosts officially recommended by WordPress.org, and its entire platform is built around WordPress: one-click installation, managed updates, free SSL and an integrated control panel. For a beginner launching their first WordPress site, it is arguably the easiest option available.

### How Much Does Bluehost Cost After Renewal?

The promotional price — around $2.95/mo for Basic on longer terms — applies only to your first term. After renewal, Basic typically costs around $12/mo, Choice Plus around $22/mo, and Pro over $30/mo, roughly double to triple the intro rates. Budget for the renewal price before you sign up.

### Does Bluehost Offer Free Migration?

For WordPress sites, yes. Bluehost provides a free migration plugin that moves a single WordPress site over at no cost. For non-WordPress or complex cPanel migrations, you will typically need Bluehost's paid migration service — so check which one applies to your situation before you switch.

### Is Bluehost Fast?

Adequate, but not the fastest. In our tests Bluehost's TTFB typically lands in the 450–550 ms range — perfectly usable for most WordPress sites, especially with a caching plugin — but clearly behind hosts like Hostinger (around 212 ms) or Scala (around 280 ms). If speed is your top priority, look elsewhere; if reliability and ease of use matter more, Bluehost is fine.

### Is Bluehost Owned by the Same Company As HostGator?

Yes. Both are part of the Newfold Digital group (formerly Endurance International Group), which also owns Domain.com, iPage and several other hosting brands. They are separate products with separate teams and support, but they share a corporate parent.
