## How the Business Model Works

The business model of MyLead is designed to provide flexibility and variety. The platform offers access to over **4,300 partner programs** across multiple categories, giving affiliates the chance to earn through nine different models, including Cost per Lead (CPL), Cost per Sale (CPS), Cost per Action (CPA), Pay per Install (PPI), and Cost on Delivery (COD), plus options like SMS campaigns and surveys.

The core idea is that you are paid only for measurable results, not impressions — that is what separates a CPA/CPL network from a display ad network. The main models in brief:

- **CPA (Cost per Action):** paid when a user performs a specific action, such as registering or subscribing.
- **CPL (Cost per Lead):** paid when a user submits contact details or completes a short form — often the easiest model to convert.
- **CPS (Cost per Sale):** paid when a user buys a product, either a percentage or a fixed amount.
- **PPI (Pay per Install):** paid when a user installs a mobile app.
- **COD (Cost on Delivery):** paid when an ecommerce order is delivered and accepted.

To make things easier, MyLead provides banners, deep links, smartlinks, product feeds, landing pages, and coupons. Smartlinks are especially useful for beginners: one link automatically routes a visitor to the best-matching offer for their geo, removing most of the guesswork from campaign setup.

## Offer Types and Verticals in Detail

MyLead's catalog spans a wide range of verticals, though its strongest areas are lead generation and performance.

**Email submit and registration offers** are the entry point for most MyLead publishers. These are classic CPL offers where a user enters an email address or fills out a short form. Payouts per lead are modest — typically low single digits depending on the geo — but conversion rates are high, which makes them forgiving for new affiliates.

**Finance, insurance, and trading offers** sit at the premium end. Leads in these verticals pay noticeably more because advertisers value qualified leads highly. Competition is higher, and approval for premium offers is not always automatic.

**App installs (PPI)** are a strong category because mobile traffic converts cleanly. **Ecommerce and CPS offers** earn a share of the sale instead of a flat fee — they pay less often but can pay much more per conversion. **Sweepstakes, surveys, and COD offers** round out the catalog.

One honest caveat: while MyLead lists thousands of partner programs, the truly active, high-paying inventory at any moment is a smaller subset. The smart approach is to filter by your traffic's geo and test a handful of offers per vertical before scaling what converts.

## The MyLead Referral Program

Additionally, MyLead offers a **referral program**, where affiliates earn **5% of the revenue** generated by anyone who joins using their referral link. This creates an extra passive income stream on top of direct commissions.

The referral model is worth understanding honestly. Five percent of a referred publisher's *network revenue* — the commission MyLead earns from their activity — is not the same as 5% of the publisher's earnings. So the referral program is a real but typically small income stream, best treated as a bonus rather than the main reason to join.

For publishers who run YouTube channels, blogs, or communities about making money online, the referral program adds a useful secondary revenue line. There is no cost and no downside to sharing a referral link, so tick that box — just do not build your business plan around it.
