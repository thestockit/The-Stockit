# 5 Best Free E-Commerce Platforms To Sell Your Products Online

Thanks to **e-commerce**, it's now possible to buy and sell products from any corner of the world at any time. Online shopping has not only **revolutionized the customer experience** but also **reshaped how businesses operate**. Instead of visiting a physical store, buyers can simply order from platforms like **Amazon, eBay, Shopify, or Etsy** within minutes.

In this guide, we'll explore the **top 5 e-commerce platforms** where you can sell your products for free (or with minimal investment), what "free" really means, and how to pick the right one for your business.

Store-builders like Shopify, BigCommerce, and Wix offer free trials but charge a monthly subscription after that, while WooCommerce is free software that still needs hosting and a domain. Pick a marketplace for built-in traffic with fees per sale, or a store-builder for brand control and predictable monthly costs.

## What "Free" Really Means

The uncomfortable truth: **nobody runs a marketplace or store platform as a charity.** Every platform makes money somehow, and the only question is how it gets paid — upfront, per sale, or both.

**Marketplace-first platforms** (Amazon, eBay, Etsy) charge **per transaction**. Listing is free or nearly free, but each sale generates a referral or final value fee plus payment processing. The trade-off is real traffic: you don't build an audience, you tap into theirs — but the cost comes out of each sale, squeezing your margin.

**Store-builder platforms** (Shopify, BigCommerce, Wix, Square Online) charge a **monthly subscription**, often with a free trial so you can build before you buy. You own the storefront, the URL, and the branding — but you must bring the traffic, and **hosting, a domain, and premium themes or apps** add up.

And there's a third category: **free software that still isn't free.** **WooCommerce** has no license fee, but it runs on WordPress and needs **hosting** (typically a few dollars to tens per month), a **domain name** (about **$10-15 a year**), and often paid themes and plugins. The software is free; the running costs are not.

## 1) Amazon

With over **3.13 billion monthly visitors (2022)**, Amazon is the **largest and most trusted e-commerce marketplace** worldwide.

### Why Sell on Amazon?

- Market share of **47%** in the U.S.
- Opportunities to diversify into **fashion, tech, and groceries**.
- Seller-friendly tools like **Fulfilled by Amazon (FBA)**.
- Huge customer base with **high trust factor**.

> Amazon is the **best platform to sell products online for free**, especially if you're looking for high traffic and credibility.

But "free" needs a footnote. The **Individual plan** charges only a per-item fee when you sell; the **Professional plan** (typically around **$39.99 per month**) adds bulk listings and advertising. Most categories also carry a **referral fee** — typically around **15%** — and **FBA** adds fulfillment and storage costs. For a beginner, the smart play is to start on the Individual plan and learn the fee math on real orders before scaling.

## 2) eBay

eBay receives **3 billion monthly visits**, making it the **second-largest e-commerce platform** after Amazon.

### Why Sell on eBay?

- Active in **190+ markets** since 1995.
- Perfect for selling **unique, rare, or second-hand items**.
- Easy setup for both casual and professional sellers.

> If your products are **niche or collectible**, eBay is your go-to marketplace.

eBay is free to join, with a limited number of **free listings per month** (typically around **250**) before small **insertion fees** apply. What bites instead is the **final value fee** — a percentage of the total sale, including shipping, with typical all-in costs in the **13-15% range**. For second-hand and niche items that's often still the best deal in e-commerce, because you reach buyers actively searching for exactly what you sell.

## 3) Etsy

Founded in 2005, **Etsy** has grown into a hub for **handmade goods, artwork, antiques, and collectibles**.

- Hosts **96.3 million active buyers** and **7.5 million sellers**.
- Generates **$4.97 billion+ annual sales**.
- Beginner-friendly with excellent customer support.

> Etsy is ideal if you're creative and want to sell **handmade or vintage items** online.

Etsy charges a **small listing fee** per item (expiring after four months), a **transaction fee** — typically around **6.5%** — and **payment processing** of roughly **3% plus a small fixed fee**, landing most sales in the **10-15% range**. Where Etsy shines is intent: buyers arrive looking for something specific and unique, so **handmade makers and small studios** often command better margins than on a crowded general marketplace.

## Shopify

**Shopify** empowers individuals and businesses to build their own **online stores**.

- Over **144 million monthly visitors**.
- Great for **dropshipping stores**.
- Average monthly income for dropshippers: **€100-€500**.
- Beginner-friendly dashboard + thousands of Shopify apps.

> Shopify is perfect if you want **full control over your store** instead of depending on a marketplace.

Shopify is not free, but it's the platform most people mean by "build my own store." A **free trial** lets you build before paying, and paid plans typically start around **$29 per month**. Using **Shopify Payments** avoids an extra transaction fee; external gateways add one. Add a theme, a few apps, and a domain, and your realistic monthly cost is usually **$40-60+**. The upside: **you own the brand and the customer data**. The trade-off: you must bring the traffic.

## Beyond The Big Five: WooCommerce, Wix, Square, And Free Trials

The five platforms above aren't the whole story:

- **WooCommerce** — free, open-source software for WordPress. You pay for **hosting, a domain, and premium extensions**, typically **$10-40+ per month**. Maximum flexibility, but a steeper learning curve and you handle security and backups.
- **Wix** — a **free plan** with Wix-branded ads, plus paid tiers (typically around **$23+/month**) for a custom domain and no ads. The drag-and-drop builder gets a store online in an afternoon.
- **Square (Square Online)** — free to start, with typical **payment processing around 2.6-3.5%**. Ideal if you already sell in person and want the same catalog online.
- **BigCommerce** — a **free trial** and pricing comparable to Shopify, but with **no transaction fees on its own payment gateway** on many plans and stronger features for large catalogs.
- **Marketplace-first options** — consider niche marketplaces where your buyers already hang out: Walmart Marketplace, Faire for wholesale, Facebook Marketplace for local sales, and category-specific sites. Niche marketplaces usually have lower competition and more qualified buyers.

## Hidden Costs: The Fine Print Nobody Advertises

The costs that surprise new sellers most:

- **Payment processing.** Typically **2.6-3.5%** plus a small fixed fee per transaction, whether called "processing" or hidden inside a marketplace fee.
- **Renewal price jumps.** Introductory rates rise at renewal — a "$29/month" plan can become "$49/month" or more.
- **Premium themes and apps.** Reviews, abandoned-cart, and email apps add up to **$10-50+ per month**.
- **Ads and promotions.** Marketplace "visibility" often means sponsored listings; paid traffic can be your biggest expense.
- **Fulfillment and storage.** FBA-style programs charge per unit and per month, quietly eating into slow-moving inventory.
- **Returns and chargebacks.** You lose the fee and the product — factor in a realistic return rate.

The honest way to compare platforms: **model your own numbers.** Run each platform's fee schedule against your average order value, volume, and margins. A platform that looks "expensive" on fees can be cheaper in total if it brings more sales.

## How To Choose The Right Platform For Your Business

There's no single "best" free platform — there's the best one **for you**:

- **Handmade, vintage, or personalized goods** → **Etsy**.
- **Everyday products for the mass market** → **Amazon**.
- **Used, rare, or collectible items** → **eBay**.
- **Building a real brand with your own store** → **Shopify, BigCommerce, or Wix**.
- **Already selling in person** → **Square**.
- **Home improvement or DIY products** → **The Home Depot Marketplace**.

A practical strategy: **start with one marketplace to validate the product**, then add your own store once you have proof of demand and some margin for marketing. Marketplaces teach you pricing and customer expectations cheaply — then you graduate to a store you control.

## FAQs

### Is it really free to sell on Amazon?

Yes and no. The **Individual plan** is free to list and charges only a per-item fee when you sell. The **Professional plan** carries a monthly subscription (typically around **$39.99**), and most sales incur a referral fee of around **15%** plus any FBA costs. "Free to start" is accurate; "free to run" is not.

### What are the typical transaction fees on eBay?

For most categories, the **final value fee** plus payment processing lands in the typical **13-15%** range of the total sale, including shipping. Basic sellers also get a limited number of **free listings per month** before insertion fees apply.

### Is Etsy really cheaper than other marketplaces?

Etsy's total fees are comparable — a small listing fee, a typical **~6.5% transaction fee**, and **~3% payment processing** — usually landing in the **10-15%** combined range. What makes Etsy "cheaper" is **buyer intent**: shoppers arrive looking for handmade and unique items, so sellers can charge higher prices and waste less on marketing.

### Do I have to pay a monthly fee for Shopify?

After the **free trial**, yes. Paid plans typically start around **$29 per month**, and your real cost is usually higher once you add a domain, a premium theme, apps, and payment processing. Budget realistically before you build.

### Can I use more than one platform at once?

Absolutely. A common play is to **list on a marketplace like Amazon or Etsy** for existing traffic while **running your own Shopify or Wix store** for brand-building and repeat customers. Keep inventory and pricing consistent across channels, and remember each channel adds its own fees and admin.
