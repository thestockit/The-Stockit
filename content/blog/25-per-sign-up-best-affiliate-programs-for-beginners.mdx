## How $25-per-sign-up programs work

A per-sign-up affiliate program is the simplest payout model in affiliate marketing. Instead of earning a percentage of a sale or a commission on a subscription, you earn a flat, fixed fee every time someone completes a specific action through your link — usually creating a free account or starting a free trial.

For the advertiser, this model is pure user acquisition. A company like Robinhood or SEMRush is willing to pay affiliates because a free user who registers today may convert into a paying customer tomorrow — or may simply become part of a user base the company can grow, engage and eventually monetize. You get paid for the registration, and the advertiser takes on the risk of whether that user ever pays.

This is why the model is ideal for beginners. There is no sales pressure. You do not have to convince anyone to open their wallet, compare prices, or complete a checkout flow. The barrier to entry for the user is close to zero, which means conversion rates are typically far higher than in a straight sales commission program. In practice, if you send targeted, relevant traffic to a per-sign-up offer, you will generally see a sign-up far more often than you would see a sale.

The catch is the flip side of the same coin: because a free sign-up is so easy, the payout per action is usually small. As you will see in the list below, most per-sign-up programs pay between $0.20 and $5 per registration. The exception is where the sign-up itself is the first step in a high-value sales process — think financial apps like Robinhood or B2B lead-gen services like Cleverly — where advertisers can afford to pay $5, $10 or even $100 because the eventual customer is worth far more.

## Payout models: sign-up, trial and lead

"Per sign-up" is an umbrella term, and the fine print matters more than the headline number. Before you join anything, understand which of these three variations you are actually being paid for:

**Free sign-up.** The user registers for a free account. This is the easiest action, so the payouts are the lowest — typically $0.20 to $2. Roblox, Grammarly, Xero and DoorDash all fall in this bucket.

**Free trial.** The user registers and activates a free trial of a paid product. This takes slightly more commitment from the user, so payouts are higher — SEMRush pays roughly $10 per trial sign-up in this list. Trial offers convert a bit lower than plain sign-ups but pay several times more, which makes them a strong middle ground.

**Qualified lead.** The user completes a form or takes a step that marks them as a genuine sales lead — often by requesting a quote, a demo or a consultation. This is a real action with real intent behind it, which is why Cleverly can pay $100 per lead. These offers convert the lowest of the three, because the user has to do something meaningful, but they pay the most per completed action.

The practical takeaway: don't compare programs by payout number alone. A $0.20 sign-up that converts at 10 percent can earn more traffic-for-traffic than a $100 lead offer that converts at 0.1 percent. Match the model to your audience's level of intent, not just to the biggest number on the page.

## How to promote these programs (without burning out)

The biggest mistake beginners make is signing up for ten programs and then posting links everywhere with zero strategy. These programs are easy to join — the hard part is sending the right traffic. Here is the promotion playbook that works for beginners.

**Start with one niche and two or three programs.** Pick an audience you know something about — gamers, students, small business owners, beginner investors — and promote only the programs that genuinely serve that audience. A focused site or channel converts dramatically better than a scattered one.

**Build content that helps first, sells second.** A "best password managers for families" guide will convert 1Password far better than a page that just says "sign up here." Tutorials, comparisons, roundups, and how-to content warm up the reader, and the sign-up link becomes a natural next step rather than an interruption.

**Use the free tools you are already promoting.** If you are promoting Grammarly or SEMRush, use them in your work and say so. Screenshots, real results, and honest "here is what I actually use" content build the trust that makes sign-ups happen.

## Risks and pitfalls to watch

Per-sign-up affiliate marketing is low-risk compared to most online business models, but it is not risk-free. Here is what can go wrong and how to avoid it.

**You promote to the wrong audience.** This is the number one killer. A $100 Cleverly lead offer promoted to gamers will earn nothing; a $0.20 Roblox offer promoted to CFOs will also earn nothing. Every program in this guide has a natural audience — respect it.

**Cookie windows and attribution.** As we noted above, short cookies mean timing matters. If your readers click and sign up later, you lose the referral. Place links where the sign-up decision actually happens, and remember that some networks also attribute differently across devices.

**Payout thresholds and minimums.** Most affiliate networks do not pay out on day one. They hold earnings until you cross a payout threshold (often $25–$100) and until a payment cycle closes. A couple of sign-ups here and there can leave your balance below threshold for months — that is normal, but budget for it, and do not treat your first payout as the measure of whether the program works.

<Callout type="warning" title="Check the payout threshold before you join">
Some networks won't pay you a cent until your balance reaches a minimum threshold, and if you never reach it, the balance can sit there indefinitely. A $20 balance below a $100 threshold is effectively $20 you cannot touch. Read the network's payment terms before you invest months of promotion into a program.
</Callout>

**Strict promotion rules.** Financial and B2B offers like Robinhood and Cleverly often prohibit pay-per-click advertising on brand terms, require disclosure, and monitor where their links appear. Violations can get your commission revoked or your account closed — so read the program terms, and never spam links in comments, forums, or WhatsApp groups.

**The "passive" myth.** These programs are called passive income because they can keep paying after the work is done, not because there is no work. Someone has to create the content, answer the questions, and keep showing up. Expect real effort in exchange for the eventual passive phase.

**Never pay to join.** Legitimate affiliate programs are free to join. If anyone asks you to pay an "activation fee" or "membership fee" to access sign-up offers, walk away — that is the signature of a scam, not a program.

## Choosing a program: a simple checklist

Before you sign up for any affiliate program, run it through this checklist. If it fails more than one item, move on.

- **Is the payout worth the effort?** Divide the payout by how hard the action is. A $0.20 sign-up is only worth it if you have real traffic; a $100 lead is only worth it if you have a precise audience.
- **Do you understand what action you are paid for?** Free sign-up, trial, or qualified lead — and what exactly counts as a conversion?
- **Does the audience actually fit?** Will the people who read your content genuinely want this product? Be brutally honest.
- **What is the cookie window and attribution model?** Is it long enough for your traffic patterns? Does it track clicks across devices?
- **Is there a payout threshold, and can you realistically reach it?** If not, your earnings are locked up indefinitely.
- **Are the promotion rules something you can follow?** Especially if you plan to use paid ads or run giveaway-style content.
- **Is the network reputable and the signup free?** No activation fees, ever.
- **Does the program have support and resources?** Affiliate managers, banners, and tracking dashboards make your life dramatically easier.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much can a beginner earn from per-sign-up affiliate programs?

Realistically, very little in the first month or two — often under $50 — because payouts are small and traffic takes time to build. With consistent content over four to six months, a focused site can plausibly earn a few hundred dollars a month from the offers in this guide, and far more if a high-payout program like Cleverly connects with the right audience.

### Do I need money to start?

No. Every program in this guide is free to join, and you can promote them with free channels like a blog, YouTube, or social media. The only common costs are optional — a domain, hosting, or paid ads — none of which are required to begin.

### What is the difference between per sign-up and per sale commission?

Per sign-up pays a flat fee for a free registration or trial, with no purchase required — fast to earn but usually small. Per sale pays a percentage of an actual purchase, which is bigger per conversion but much harder to get. Beginners often start with sign-up offers because the conversion barrier is lower.

### How do I get paid?

Through the affiliate network that runs the program — for example, Impact Radius, CJ Affiliate, VigLink, or PartnerStack — via direct deposit, PayPal, or bank transfer depending on the network. Payments are typically issued monthly once you cross the network's payout threshold.

### Do these programs really pay per free sign-up?

Yes, when the action is defined as a free sign-up, you are paid regardless of whether the user ever buys. The catch is the payout is small precisely because the action is easy. Always read the program's terms to confirm what counts as a qualifying sign-up — some exclude certain regions, require a verified account, or change the action definition over time.
