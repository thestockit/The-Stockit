We spent several weeks testing FastComet as part of our ongoing hosting series, measuring performance from multiple locations, poking at the backup and security stack, and opening real support conversations. This review is the honest result. Every performance number is an estimate from our own tests, and where we cite industry-typical figures we say so — hosting performance varies with server load, data center and time of day, so treat everything as directional rather than guaranteed.

## Data centers: a genuinely global network, including Asia

This is the section that matters most for readers in Pakistan, India and the wider region, and it is a real FastComet strength. Most value-focused hosts give you a choice between "US" and "Europe" and call it global. FastComet operates a much wider footprint:

- **North America** — data centers on the US East and West coasts
- **Europe** — locations in the UK, Netherlands, Germany and Scandinavia
- **Asia** — including Singapore and India, plus additional regional nodes
- **Australia** — a Pacific presence for ANZ audiences

Singapore and India matter specifically because they cover the two populations this site serves best: Pakistani readers whose traffic clusters in Asia, and the broader South Asian market. A visitor in Lahore connecting to a Singapore or India node will see dramatically better TTFB than one routed to Texas or Frankfurt. In our testing, the Asia node returned numbers that were solidly acceptable for that geography — not the fastest we have seen from any host, but far better than the "fly to the US" experience that cheaper hosts impose.

The practical rule is the one we repeat in every review: **choose the data center closest to your audience, not the one that looks best on a map.** FastComet lets you select your location at checkout, which at this price point is genuinely unusual — many hosts at this tier force a single region on you.

## Backups and security: the differentiator

FastComet's headline feature deserves its own section, because it is the honest reason most people should consider this host.

**Free daily offsite backups, on every plan, with one-click restore.** In our testing, the backup ran every night without us lifting a finger, and the restore flow from the control panel worked as advertised — we rolled a test site back to a prior day's state in a few clicks. Offsite matters here: the copies live on separate infrastructure, so a full server failure or a hack that wipes your files does not wipe your backups with it. That is a genuinely meaningful safety property, and it is the single feature most hosts reserve for their expensive tiers.

The rest of the security stack is solidly modern:

- **Free SSL certificates** on every plan, auto-renewed, covering your primary domain and subdomains.
- **A free security suite** including malware scanning and removal on shared plans — many hosts sell this as a $20+/year add-on, FastComet bundles it.
- **Per-account isolation** on shared infrastructure, which limits the blast radius of any single compromise.
- **System-level firewalls and brute-force protection** configured at the hosting layer rather than left to your plugins.

Two honest caveats. The security suite protects the platform, but nothing replaces a hardening plugin and sensible admin passwords on your own WordPress install. And the free SSL is domain-validation (DV) only, which is right for 99% of sites but not for organizations that need organization-validation certificates.

FastComet offers **free website migration**, and unlike hosts that hand you a plugin and wish you luck, FastComet's team does the moving. You open a migration request, hand over access to your existing host, and their staff transfers files, databases, email accounts and SSL across — typically within a day or two in our experience.

Very complex setups — custom cron jobs, unusual configurations, exceptionally large media libraries — may need a support check-in first, and the free migration covers a reasonable number of sites per account.
