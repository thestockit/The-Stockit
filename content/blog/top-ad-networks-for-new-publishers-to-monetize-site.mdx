# Top Ad Networks for New Publishers to Monetize Their Websites

<Callout type="info" title="The short version">Google AdSense is the natural first stop, but its strict content review and $100 payout threshold leave many new publishers searching elsewhere. This guide covers the real alternatives for beginners — RevenueHits, Ezoic, AdMaven, PropellerAds, and Yllix — plus premium networks like Mediavine and Raptive that you can grow into. If you have modest traffic, start with a no-minimum network and graduate to higher-paying display networks as your audience grows.</Callout>

Every publisher dreams of turning their hard work into a steady source of income, but getting started can be challenging. As a new publisher, one of the biggest hurdles is meeting the strict requirements of top-level ad networks like Google AdSense. With limited content and traffic, approval often feels out of reach. The good news is that there are several AdSense alternatives designed specifically for beginners, with minimal requirements and reliable payouts. These networks give you a chance to monetize your website while you continue building traffic and authority.

In this article, we highlight the best ad networks for new publishers that are easy to join, user-friendly, and proven to reward publishers fairly. We also walk through the traffic thresholds you should realistically expect, the ad formats worth testing, and the warning signs to avoid so you can pick the right partner for your site.

## How to Read the Numbers in This Guide

Before comparing networks, it helps to understand the metrics that appear on every publisher dashboard. These numbers are how you will judge whether a network is working for you.

**RPM** (revenue per thousand pageviews) is the amount you earn for every 1,000 pageviews on your site, across all your ad units. It is the closest thing to a single "how much did I make" number and the figure most publishers compare.

**CPM** (cost per mille) is what an advertiser pays per 1,000 ad impressions. Your CPM is set by supply and demand: finance content viewed in the US and UK commands far more than general content viewed in lower-income regions.

**CPC** (cost per click) means you are paid each time a visitor clicks an ad, rather than simply viewing it. Contextual networks like AdSense lean heavily on this model.

**Fill rate** is the share of your ad impressions that actually sell. A network that fills 95% of your impressions at a modest CPM can easily out-earn one that fills 50% at a high CPM.

**Payout threshold** is the minimum balance you must reach before the network will send you money. For beginners this matters more than it looks: a $100 threshold with small daily earnings can mean months before your first payment.

**Payment terms** describe how often and how quickly networks pay, typically every 30 to 60 days after the month ends. Many premium networks are "net-30," meaning earnings from January arrive toward the end of February.

Throughout this guide, figures for RPM, traffic thresholds, and payout minimums are illustrative and hedged. They reflect typical industry observations, not guarantees — and every network's terms have shifted over time, so always confirm current numbers on the platform's own site before you commit.

## The Traffic Reality: What Networks Actually Ask For

The single biggest source of confusion for new publishers is traffic requirements, because they vary enormously between network types.

Google AdSense publishes no hard traffic minimum. Its approval process is a **quality review** of your content and policy compliance rather than an audience-size test. In practice, however, a brand-new site with a handful of monthly visits and thin content is often rejected. AdSense wants to see a real website — useful content, clear navigation, a privacy policy, and a reasonable number of published pages — even if your readership is still small.

Premium managed networks are the opposite. Companies like Mediavine and Raptive sell your inventory to large advertisers and take an active role in placement and optimization, so they only accept sites with meaningful, consistent audiences. Their thresholds have changed over the years, but a **typical starting point for these premium display networks is around 50,000 sessions per month**, with more demanding programs asking for roughly twice that.

That is exactly why they are the most realistic starting point for a new publisher.

## Google AdSense: The Benchmark for Contextual Ads

Google AdSense is where most publishers start, and for good reason. It is a contextual advertising network: Google scans your page content and serves ads that match your topic and your audience. That automation makes it remarkably simple — paste a code snippet into your site, choose your ad formats, and Google handles targeting, fill, and payment.

The network pays on a mix of **CPC and CPM**, so you earn both when visitors click and when advertisers bid on your impressions. Typical display RPMs are modest — often in the single digits of dollars, and lower for general-interest content outside Tier 1 geographies — but the demand pool is enormous, which keeps fill rates high.

The trade-offs are the approval review and the payout threshold. AdSense reviews your site for policy compliance and content quality, and rejected applications must be fixed and resubmitted. The **$100 minimum payout** also means a low-earning site can wait several months for its first check, with payments issued roughly a month after you cross the threshold.

## Ezoic: AI-Powered Optimization for Growing Sites

Ezoic is a Google-certified ad exchange network and one of the fastest-growing platforms in the industry. It is widely considered the best AdSense alternative in terms of revenue potential. What sets Ezoic apart is its artificial intelligence-driven ad tester, which optimizes ad placements automatically based on user behavior and preferences. This ensures higher engagement and better revenue for publishers.

The platform serves over 1.7 billion ad impressions monthly and offers additional learning resources to help new publishers. Ezoic accepts websites without a strict traffic requirement, making it easy for beginners to join, and its Access Now program is specifically designed for smaller sites. The minimum payout is $20, and payments can be withdrawn via PayPal, Payoneer, Wise, bank transfer, or check. Since Ezoic is a Google Partner, new publishers must comply with Google Publisher Policies.

The main thing to understand about Ezoic is that it is not just an ad network — it is a platform. You add your site through their system, and they run ads, testing, and in some cases hosting. Publishers who want maximum control sometimes find the setup unusual, but for beginners the automation is the point: Ezoic runs the experiments so you do not have to.

## Mediavine: Premium Display for Lifestyle Publishers

Mediavine is a premium display advertising network with a strong reputation among content sites, especially in the food, lifestyle, parenting, and DIY niches. Unlike the self-serve networks above, Mediavine manages your ad placements for you, which typically produces higher effective RPMs because its team optimizes layouts, viewability, and demand.

The catch is access. Mediavine historically required around **50,000 sessions per month** to apply, and it reviews your content quality as part of approval. That is a significant bar for a new publisher, but it is achievable for a site that has been publishing consistently for a year or two. Payments are typically issued on a net-30 basis once you meet the monthly threshold.

Mediavine is not a realistic starting point for a brand-new site, but it belongs on your roadmap. Publishers who graduate from a beginner network to Mediavine often see their per-visitor earnings jump, because premium brand demand pays far more than the push and popunder inventory of the no-minimum networks.

## Raptive: The Premium Tier for Larger Sites

Raptive, formerly known as AdThrive, sits at the top of the publisher food chain alongside Mediavine. It targets large, established content sites and historically asked for roughly **100,000 pageviews per month** to apply, with a strong emphasis on content quality and audience loyalty.

What Raptive offers in exchange is some of the highest CPMs available to independent publishers, along with a full service team that handles ad management, revenue strategy, and technical setup. Its demand comes from brand and programmatic advertisers who pay a premium for engaged, verified audiences.

For a new publisher, Raptive is a destination rather than a starting line. It is worth knowing the name, because many publishers set the traffic targets above with exactly this tier in mind. If your niche is valuable and your audience is loyal, reaching Raptive's bar can be transformative for your income.

## Adsterra: A Global Network With No Traffic Minimum

Adsterra is a global advertising network that is unusually welcoming to new publishers. It has no strict traffic minimum and accepts sites in most niches and geographies, which makes it a popular first network for beginners whose audiences are not concentrated in Tier 1 countries.

Adsterra supports **CPM, CPC, and CPA** payment models and offers a wide range of formats, including display banners, native ads, social bar, popunders, and push notifications. Its minimum payout is $5, with payments available through PayPal, Payoneer, and various other methods. Because it serves global demand, it is a strong choice when your traffic comes from regions that larger networks undervalue.

## PropellerAds: The Push and Popunder Specialist

PropellerAds is one of the most popular ad networks, with a reputation for delivering massive global traffic. Founded in 2011, the network now serves more than 12 million ad impressions daily and reaches over one billion users monthly. This makes it one of the biggest alternative traffic sources for advertisers worldwide.

PropellerAds works on a **CPM model**, meaning publishers are paid for every 1,000 impressions they generate. The network supports multiple ad formats, including push notifications, in-page push, popunders, interstitials, and direct click ads. With a low minimum payout of $5 and flexible payment methods such as PayPal, Skrill, Webmoney, and wire transfer, PropellerAds remains one of the most reliable choices for new publishers.

The realistic expectation with PropellerAds is that push and popunder formats earn less per impression than premium display — typical effective CPMs are modest — but the network compensates with high volume and near-total fill. For a beginner with growing traffic, that consistency is often more valuable than chasing the highest possible rate.

## RevenueHits: Equal Payouts in Every Region

RevenueHits tops the list as one of the most beginner-friendly ad networks available today. It is a self-service platform that allows publishers to fully monetize their websites through advanced ad-serving technology. The platform processes more than 2 billion impressions daily and has over 20,000 publishers worldwide.

One of the unique advantages of RevenueHits is that it offers equal payout rates across all regions. Whether your traffic comes from Tier 1 countries like the United States and the United Kingdom, or Tier 3 countries like India and Pakistan, you can expect the same rates. The platform also supports multiple ad formats, including pop ads, push notifications, in-page push, interstitials, and content blockers. The minimum payout is only $5, and payments can be received through PayPal, Payoneer, Bitcoin, wire transfer, and other global payment options.

That equal-rate policy matters if your audience is spread across many countries. Most networks price impressions by geography, which punishes publishers with global but not Tier 1 traffic. RevenueHits removes that penalty, making it a sensible choice for internationally focused sites.

## AdMaven: Flexible Models for New Publishers

AdMaven is a premium ad network that supports **CPM, CPC, and CPA** payment models. With more than 1 billion ad impressions per month, it provides an excellent opportunity for publishers who were rejected by AdSense or want to diversify their income. The platform is extremely user-friendly, supports all verticals, and does not require a minimum traffic threshold to get started.

Publishers can monetize their websites through multiple ad formats, including pop ads, push notifications, in-page push, interstitials, and content blockers. AdMaven's minimum payout is $5, and it supports a wide range of payment methods, including PayPal, Payoneer, Bitcoin, and wire transfer. The only limitation is that under-construction websites are not accepted — your site needs to be live and working to join.

Because AdMaven supports three payment models, it is a good testing ground for learning which monetization approach suits your audience. Try different formats, watch your RPM, and let the numbers tell you what works.

## Yllix: Instant Approval and the Lowest Payout

Yllix is another excellent choice for new publishers, especially because of its instant approval system and extremely low payout threshold. This global **CPM, CPC, and CPA** advertising network accepts minimal traffic and still pays consistently. Publishers can withdraw earnings starting from just $1, with payment cycles available daily, weekly, or monthly.

The platform supports a variety of ad formats such as banners, redirects, sliders, popups, popunders, and direct links. Yllix works for both adult and non-adult websites, although revenue depends largely on traffic quality and audience location. Additionally, Yllix offers a referral program where affiliates can earn up to $100 for every new active user they bring in.

The $1 payout threshold and instant approval make Yllix the fastest way for an absolute beginner to see real money land in their account. It is not the highest-earning network on this list, but as a confidence builder and a cash-flow starter, it is hard to beat.

## Ad Formats: Match the Unit to Your Audience

The format you choose matters as much as the network, because formats map to different types of audiences and different tolerance levels.

**Display banners and native ads** are the most publisher-friendly. They are unobtrusive, blend with your layout, and are what premium networks like Mediavine and Raptive specialize in. Their RPMs depend heavily on niche and geography.

**In-page push and push notifications** appear as browser notifications. They are less visually intrusive than popups and work well on mobile, which is why networks like PropellerAds and Adsterra push them hard.

**Popunders** open a new tab or window behind the visitor's current page. They have a reputation for being aggressive, and advertisers pay accordingly — but used heavily, they can hurt user experience and bounce rates.

**Interstitials** are full-screen ads shown at natural transition points, such as between pages or articles. They are effective but must be handled carefully so they do not block content.

**Direct link ads** take the visitor straight to an advertiser's landing page. They convert well for some niches but are the least brand-safe option.

A sensible beginner strategy is to start with one or two formats on a low-minimum network, watch your RPM and your site's engagement, and only add more units once you understand their effect on your readers.

## Payout Minimums and Payment Methods at a Glance

Payout thresholds range from $1 on Yllix to $100 on AdSense, and that difference is enormous for a new publisher. At a typical beginner RPM, a $100 threshold can mean two to three months of waiting; a $5 or $1 threshold means your first payment arrives almost immediately after you start earning.

Payment methods also vary. PayPal and Payoneer are the most common, but some networks add Bitcoin, wire transfer, Skrill, Wise, or bank transfer. If you live in a region where PayPal is restricted, check the supported methods before you join — it is a common reason publishers switch networks later.

Finally, understand net terms. "Net-30" means earnings from one month are paid about 30 days after that month closes. Some lower-tier networks pay faster, and a few even offer daily withdrawals. Faster isn't always better — speed often correlates with lower ad quality — but for cash flow it is worth knowing what you are signing up for.

## Approval Tips for New Publishers

Whichever network you target, a few basics make approval far more likely:

1. **Publish real content before you apply.** A dozen or more genuinely useful articles beats a handful of thin pages. Networks and Google reviewers want to see a website a reader would trust.
2. **Add the standard pages.** A privacy policy, about page, and contact page signal a legitimate site. Most networks require a privacy policy anyway.
3. **Drive some organic traffic first.** A small but consistent stream of visitors from search or social shows the site is alive. Even a few hundred monthly visits is enough for most no-minimum networks.
4. **Keep your layout clean.** A cluttered page with too many competing widgets looks like a spam site. Minimal, readable design helps both approval and ad performance.
5. **Never click your own ads.** It is the fastest way to be banned, and the click will be invalidated anyway.
6. **Fix your site speed.** Slow pages hurt both approval and RPMs. Compress images, use caching, and test on mobile.

## Warnings: Policies, Ad Blockers, and Ad Density

<Callout type="warning" title="Warning">Do not violate ad policies. Clicking your own ads, using deceptive layouts, or showing ads on prohibited content can get your account banned and your earnings withheld permanently — no network will negotiate. Also remember that ad blockers are a real cost: a meaningful share of visitors never sees your ads, especially in tech-savvy audiences, and that lost demand never shows up in your dashboards. Finally, resist the urge to stack ad units. Sites plastered with ads earn less over time because bounce rates climb, viewability falls, and advertisers bid less on cluttered pages.</Callout>

Three warnings deserve emphasis because they are where beginners get hurt:

**Policy compliance is non-negotiable.** Every network on this list has a terms-of-service agreement, and violations are handled with zero tolerance. Invalid clicks, incentive programs that push traffic into ads, and prohibited content categories are common ban reasons. Read the terms before you monetize, not after.

**Ad blockers quietly cut your revenue.** Depending on your audience and region, a significant percentage of visitors may never render an ad at all. You cannot eliminate ad blockers, but you can reduce their impact by avoiding the most aggressive formats (popunders especially) and by using formats that are less likely to be filtered.

**Ad density has a ceiling.** More ads is not more money. Google and most networks penalize pages that are "above the fold heavy" with ads, and users hate them. The general principle is to keep ad share below a third of your on-screen space and always make content the focus.

## How to Choose the Right Network for Your Site

Here is a simple decision framework based on where you are today:

- **Brand-new site, under ~10,000 monthly pageviews:** Start with a no-minimum network such as Adsterra, PropellerAds, RevenueHits, AdMaven, or Yllix. Yllix is the fastest for your first payout; PropellerAds and Adsterra are solid volume choices.
- **Modest traffic but quality content:** Apply to Google AdSense and Ezoic. AdSense gives you trustworthy contextual demand, and Ezoic adds automated optimization.
- **Growing consistently toward 50,000+ monthly sessions:** Start planning your Mediavine application. Tighten your content quality, speed, and ad layout before you submit.
- **Large, established audience:** Raptive is the premium tier to target for the highest CPMs, with Mediavine as the alternative if you do not meet its bar.

Remember that you do not have to pick one network forever. Many publishers stack a display network with a push or popunder network to maximize fill. Just watch your page speed and your bounce rate while you do it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a New Website Need Thousands of Visitors to Join?

No. Several networks on this list — Adsterra, PropellerAds, RevenueHits, AdMaven, and Yllix — accept sites with minimal or no traffic, provided the site is live and policy-compliant. Premium networks like Mediavine and Raptive do require substantial traffic, typically in the tens of thousands of sessions per month, but those are stepping stones rather than starting points.

### What Is a Good RPM for a New Publisher?

Typical beginner RPMs are modest — often in the low single digits of dollars for display ads, with push and popunder formats generally lower. Niche, geography, and ad quality matter more than the network name. Treat any published RPM as illustrative and compare networks on your own site over a month or two before judging them.

### Can I Run More Than One Ad Network at the Same Time?

Yes, and many publishers do. A common stack is a display network for your main content alongside a push or popunder network for fill. Just avoid stacking multiple units in the same space, watch your page speed, and make sure each network's terms allow you to run other providers.

### How Quickly Will I Get Paid?

It depends on the network and the payment cycle. Networks with $1 to $5 minimums often pay within days of you reaching the threshold, with cycles running daily, weekly, or monthly. Networks like AdSense pay roughly a month after you cross a $100 threshold. Premium networks typically operate on net-30 terms.

### Are These Ad Networks Legitimate?

All of the networks named in this guide are long-established, widely used platforms. "Legitimate," however, does not mean "guaranteed to pay well" — earnings depend on your traffic and niche. You should still read each network's terms, check its current payout requirements, and be wary of any platform that promises unrealistic RPMs or asks you to click your own ads.

RevenueHits, Ezoic, AdMaven, PropellerAds, and Yllix are some of the best AdSense alternatives for new publishers. They are easy to join, have minimal requirements, and provide flexible payment options. Google AdSense remains the benchmark to grow toward, while Mediavine and Raptive are the premium tiers worth building for.
