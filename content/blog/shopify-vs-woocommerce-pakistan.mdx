# Shopify vs WooCommerce for Pakistan: Which Store Platform Is Right for You?

<Callout type="info" title="The short version">
Both **Shopify and WooCommerce** can sell successfully in Pakistan, but they differ sharply in **cost, payments, hosting and control**. Shopify is a fully managed, USD-priced subscription that gets you live fast with hosting included. WooCommerce is free software that runs on hosting you arrange yourself, priced in PKR, with full control over payments, checkout and code. **Shopify wins on simplicity; WooCommerce wins on cost at scale and payment flexibility.** The right pick depends on your catalogue size, budget, and how much technical work you want to take on.
</Callout>

If you are launching an online store in Pakistan, the first platform question is almost always the same: **Shopify or WooCommerce?** Ask five people and you'll get five confident answers, most based on somebody else's market, payment setup, and exchange rate. That last part matters in Pakistan more than almost anywhere else, because Shopify bills in **US dollars** while WooCommerce keeps most of your costs in **PKR**. If you want help building either one, our [**e-commerce services hub**](/services/e-commerce) is a good starting point.

## What Each Platform Actually Is

**Shopify is a hosted platform.** You rent space on Shopify's own infrastructure, which handles hosting, security, uptime, and the core software running your store. You pay a **monthly subscription in US dollars** for a storefront that is live within hours, one-click themes, and a maintained checkout. The trade-off: you operate inside Shopify's rules, and your payment options are limited to what the Shopify app store offers for Pakistan.

**WooCommerce is a free plugin for WordPress** that turns a WordPress website into a full e-commerce store. You're responsible for hosting, domain, SSL, backups, updates and security — which is exactly why it costs less and gives you more control. You own the code, choose your hosting, and can connect **any payment method** that works in Pakistan, not just the ones an app store approves. In short, they sit at different points on the trade-off between **convenience and control**.

A typical Shopify starter store runs roughly **PKR 130,000–160,000 in year one** at a typical exchange rate of around **280–300 PKR/USD**; a comparable WooCommerce store on good local hosting lands around **PKR 60,000–80,000**. These are *illustrative* figures, not quotes — but the shape is consistent: **WooCommerce is generally cheaper to run, especially as your catalogue grows.**

## Cost Comparison in PKR: What You Actually Pay

The two platforms hide their costs in very different places.

**Shopify** charges a monthly subscription in **US dollars**, so your cost moves with the exchange rate — a real consideration for a business budgeting in PKR. Typical pricing puts the entry Basic plan around **$29–39/month**, with mid-tier plans around **$105/month** — at roughly **280–300 PKR/USD**, about **PKR 8,000–12,000 per month**. On top of that, Shopify takes a **small transaction fee** on every order unless you use Shopify Payments, which is **not available in Pakistan**. Most local stores use third-party payment apps, and combined transaction costs can quietly take **2–4% of every order**. The subscription includes hosting, security and maintenance, but you pay it **every month, forever**, in a currency that is not your own.

**WooCommerce** is free, but you pay for **hosting, a domain and an SSL certificate**. Good Pakistani-friendly hosting costs roughly **PKR 1,000–5,000 a month**, a decent **.pk or .com domain** runs around **PKR 2,500–4,000 per year**, and SSL is free with most modern hosts. A one-time premium theme costs **PKR 8,000–30,000**, or nothing if you start with a free one. You own everything, and there are **no transaction fees charged by the platform itself**; your gateway charges its rates, but the platform never takes a cut on top.

The honest summary: **WooCommerce is usually cheaper to run at scale, but Shopify is simpler to start and includes hosting.** If you already know you want [**Shopify development**](/shopify-development) or [**CMS development**](/cms-development), jump straight to those pages.

## The Payment Gateway Reality in Pakistan

This is where Pakistan-specific experience matters most, and where most generic advice goes wrong — because it was written for markets where **Shopify Payments** and **Stripe** are available. They are not available to Pakistani sellers.

With **WooCommerce**, you have full control. We can connect **JazzCash, Easypaisa, bank transfer and card payments** directly, plus build custom order flows — for example, a dealer network that orders on WhatsApp. Because there is no gatekeeper on payments, a local developer can integrate essentially anything that exists in the Pakistani payment ecosystem, and you can swap gateways when a better rate appears. For large catalogues and custom requirements, a [**custom web development**](/web-development) build gives you the same flexibility without platform limits.

<Callout type="warning" title="Payment gateways are the make-or-break decision">
The most common complaint we hear from Pakistani sellers who picked the wrong platform is not about design or speed — it's about **payments**. On Shopify, you are limited to the payment apps available in the app store for Pakistan. Good local options exist, but you work within Shopify's rules, and some setups are much easier on an open platform. Before you commit, list the exact payment methods your customers will use — JazzCash, Easypaisa, bank transfer, cards, cash on delivery — and confirm each works on the platform you're considering. A store that can't accept the way Pakistanis actually pay is a store that can't sell.
</Callout>

On **Shopify**, payment apps that connect JazzCash and Easypaisa exist in the app store, and many Pakistani stores run successfully on them — but every payment route runs through Shopify's approval process, gateways occasionally get removed or require lengthy verification, and you have less room to negotiate fees.

If you are unsure what to integrate, read our guide to the [**best payment gateways for websites in Pakistan**](/blog/best-payment-gateway-for-your-website-in-pakistan) before deciding — the payment decision should shape the platform decision, not the other way around.

## Hosting, Speed and Who Maintains What

**Shopify** hosts everything for you — fast, secure, no maintenance. Pages are served from a global CDN, uptime is Shopify's problem, and security patches and backups are simply not yours. That is genuinely convenient, especially for a small team.

**WooCommerce** runs on your own hosting, so speed and uptime depend on your host and how the site is built. That can be an advantage: a well-optimised store on a good Pakistani or international host can be very fast for local visitors. The disadvantage is that **you** are responsible for keeping it that way — a cheap shared host, too many plugins, and unoptimised images will make any WooCommerce store slow, and slow stores lose sales.

<Callout type="warning" title="Hosting is a recurring decision, not a one-time buy">
WooCommerce's price advantage only holds if you choose **decent hosting**. The cheapest shared plans can be painfully slow for an online store, and slow stores destroy conversion no matter how good your products are. Budget for a **reputable shared or cloud host** with good regional connectivity, and plan for your hosting cost to rise with traffic. Pay for the hosting your store needs today, not the cheapest price you can find — a couple of thousand PKR a month is usually far cheaper than the sales a slow site loses.
</Callout>

## Ease of Use and Setup Time

For a founder who wants to be selling this week, **Shopify wins on speed.** You create an account, pick a theme, add products, connect a payment app, and you're live — often in a single day. You never touch code unless you want to, which is a genuine advantage when testing a product or launching a fashion or D2C brand.

**WooCommerce is slower to set up because it starts from zero.** You need hosting, a domain, WordPress, the WooCommerce plugin, a theme, and a payment gateway configured. A first-time seller doing it alone can easily spend a few weekends, but an experienced developer can have a store live in days with the payment flows Pakistan needs wired in from day one.

The real difference is **ongoing day-to-day work**. Shopify handles updates, backups and security for you; WooCommerce requires someone to keep WordPress, the plugin and every extension updated. If that maintenance falls on you, factor in the cost of your time.

## Customization and Control

**Shopify** themes get you a professional store quickly. You can customise a lot — colors, fonts, layout, sections, apps — but deep customisation still happens inside Shopify's rules. If you need something unusual, you either find an app that does it (and pay a monthly fee) or wait for Shopify to ship the feature.

**WooCommerce** gives you complete control over design, checkout, shipping and every piece of code. That matters if you need **bilingual Urdu + English product pages**, unusual shipping rules (COD zones, city-specific rates), or a custom checkout flow that matches how your customers buy — such as confirming orders on WhatsApp before charging. On WooCommerce, none of that is a platform limitation; it's just a development task.

**Shopify limits you to what the platform supports; WooCommerce limits you only to what you (or a developer) can build.**

## Ongoing Costs: Apps, Plugins and the Monthly Bill

The purchase price is only half the story; the **ongoing costs** are where the two diverge most sharply.

**Shopify's recurring cost is a subscription you can't avoid.** The Basic plan at roughly **PKR 8,000–12,000/month** arrives whether you sell 10 orders or 1,000. Then come the apps: many useful Shopify apps — reviews, loyalty, email, bundling — are **$10–50/month each**, billed in USD and stacking up quickly, sometimes costing more than the plan itself.

**WooCommerce's recurring cost is infrastructure plus plugins.** Most plugins are **one-time purchases** rather than subscriptions — you buy a license and use it for life, renewing only if you want ongoing updates. A typical store needs maybe **PKR 15,000–40,000** worth of premium plugins once, rather than a monthly USD bill forever.

<Callout type="warning" title="Plugin and app costs add up fast">
On Shopify, expect **monthly USD subscriptions** for anything beyond the basics — reviews, abandoned cart, email — and audit them regularly; a store can quietly accumulate thousands of rupees of monthly app fees. On WooCommerce, the equivalent features are usually **one-time plugin purchases**, but the risk is the opposite: over-buying. A handful of well-chosen plugins does the job; fifty will slow the site and cause conflicts. Review your apps or plugins every quarter and cut anything that isn't earning its keep.
</Callout>

Over a three-year horizon this compounds. Shopify's subscription and app stack is a **USD-denominated recurring cost**; WooCommerce's main costs are **PKR-denominated and mostly fixed after setup**. For a store that plans to run for years, that's a significant structural advantage for WooCommerce.

## Which One Should You Choose? A Decision Framework

There is no universally correct answer — but there is a correct answer *for your situation*.

**Choose Shopify if:**
- You want to launch fast with minimal technical work.
- You run a fashion, food or D2C brand selling a focused catalogue.
- You are happy paying a monthly fee and letting Shopify handle hosting.
- Your budget is comfortable with USD exposure and monthly app costs.

**Choose WooCommerce if:**
- You have a large catalogue, custom integrations or a dealer network.
- You want full control over payments and checkout.
- You prefer PKR-friendly hosting costs over a USD subscription.
- You need bilingual Urdu + English content or unusual shipping rules.

## FAQs

### Can I use JazzCash and Easypaisa on Shopify in Pakistan?

Yes — but through third-party payment apps from the Shopify app store, not Shopify Payments, which is not available in Pakistan. The apps work, but you're limited to what's approved in the app store. WooCommerce allows direct integration with no platform gatekeeper.

### Is WooCommerce really free?

The **plugin itself is free**, but running it isn't. You pay for hosting (typically **PKR 1,000–5,000/month**), a domain (**PKR 2,500–4,000/year**), and optionally a premium theme and plugins. A realistic first-year total is roughly **PKR 60,000–80,000** — a lot less than a comparable Shopify store, but not zero.

### Which platform is cheaper for a store that will grow large?

Over a multi-year horizon, **WooCommerce is almost always cheaper** because its main costs are PKR-denominated, mostly one-time, and don't scale with order volume the way a USD subscription plus transaction fees do.

### How fast can I get my store live on each platform?

A Shopify store can be live **within hours** — it's designed for that. A WooCommerce store typically takes **a few days to a few weeks** depending on whether you set it up yourself or work with a developer. Shopify's speed advantage narrows significantly with a professional WooCommerce setup.

### Can I move from Shopify to WooCommerce later?

Yes. You'll need to migrate products, customer data and order history, and rebuild your theme and payment setup. It's not instant, but it's a well-trodden path — many Pakistani brands start on Shopify and migrate as their catalogue grows.

## The Bottom Line

<Callout type="info" title="Bottom line">
**Shopify is the faster, simpler start; WooCommerce is the cheaper, more flexible long-term home.** The decision comes down to three questions: Are you OK paying in USD for convenience? Do you need payment methods beyond what the Shopify app store offers for Pakistan? And will your catalogue stay small, or grow into something with custom flows and a dealer network? If speed and simplicity win, start on Shopify. If control, PKR costs and payment flexibility win — especially for a large catalogue — build on WooCommerce. The wrong choice is the one that doesn't match how your customers actually pay and how your business actually grows.
</Callout>
