# E-commerce in Pakistan 2026: Trends Every Online Store Should Know

<Callout type="info" title="The short version">

## Why E-Commerce Is Accelerating in Pakistan

A few forces are pushing online shopping forward in Pakistan:

- **Cheaper data and better coverage** — 4G and increasingly 5G networks mean mobile browsing is the default shopping experience.
- **Local payment habits** — JazzCash and Easypaisa wallets are now a normal part of daily life, and shoppers expect them at checkout.
- **Social commerce** — Instagram and Facebook shops send millions of clicks to storefronts every month.
- **Trust is moving online** — more businesses now build proper storefronts instead of relying only on Facebook pages and WhatsApp groups.

None of this means it is easy. The market is competitive, and the stores that win are the ones built around how Pakistanis actually buy.

The headline numbers keep pointing one way. While precise figures vary by report, most independent and industry surveys describe the Pakistani e-commerce market growing at a healthy double-digit clip year over year, with mobile now accounting for the large majority of sessions and orders. Compare that with traditional retail, which still grows in single digits in most years, and it is easy to see why so many offline sellers are opening their first storefront.

That growth is concentrated, though. The big metros — Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad and Rawalpindi — still take the largest share of online orders, but tier-two cities like Faisalabad, Multan, Peshawar and Sialkot are where the fastest new shopper growth is coming from. For sellers, that means a catalogue, checkout and delivery network built for the whole country, not just two or three cities.

## The Trends That Will Define Online Stores in 2026

### 1. JazzCash and Easypaisa Checkout Is Non-Negotiable

Shoppers in Pakistan do not want to type card details into a form they do not trust. They want to tap a **JazzCash** or **Easypaisa** option, pay through an app they already use, and get a confirmation on WhatsApp.

Every store we build — whether it is a [**Shopify store**](/shopify-development) or a [**custom e-commerce build**](/web-development) — ships with local wallet checkout as standard. If your store does not accept the payment methods your customers already use, you are handing sales to competitors.

### 2. WhatsApp Ordering Is Becoming the Default

Pakistanis love to ask before they buy. "Is this available?" "Can you deliver to Lahore?" "Any discount on bulk?" — these conversations happen on WhatsApp, and the best stores handle them automatically.

That is why WhatsApp-first flows matter. Product pages should open a WhatsApp chat with the product pre-loaded, so a shopper can confirm an order in seconds. For higher volume, an [**AI agent**](/ai-agents) can answer the common questions instantly, day or night.

### 3. Urdu and Roman-Urdu Product Pages

English-only stores leave money on the table. Millions of Pakistani shoppers search and read in **Urdu, or Urdu written in Roman script**. Stores that include bilingual product descriptions, and localised sizing and delivery information, simply convert better.

### 4. Mobile-First, Budget-Friendly Design

Most orders in Pakistan happen on mid-range Android phones on a 4G connection. A store that is heavy, slow or breaks on small screens will be abandoned. Fast, mobile-first design is not a nice-to-have anymore — it is a requirement.

### 5. Paid Traffic Requires Tracking, Not Guesswork

Facebook and Instagram ads work in Pakistan, but only when every rupee is tracked to a real order. In 2026, stores that thrive pair **Google Shopping** and **paid social** with proper conversion tracking, and review PKR numbers weekly instead of relying on "likes".

## Cash on Delivery: the Payment Method That Still Rules

If you ask Pakistani shoppers how they like to pay, cash on delivery remains the most common answer — even in 2026. Reports consistently suggest that COD still accounts for well over half of all online orders in Pakistan, even as wallet payments grow quickly. For many shoppers, especially outside the big metros, paying at the door is the default way to buy online, because it requires no app, no bank account and no card.

Here is what that means in practice for a store owner:

- **COD has a real cost.** Delivery companies charge a service fee per COD order, typically a small percentage of the order value, which eats into margin on every sale.
- **COD drives returns.** Because nothing is paid at checkout, the shopper has little to lose by ordering. A product that does not match expectations, arrives late, or simply triggers buyer's remorse can be refused at the door with no penalty.
- **Cash management is harder.** You need processes to reconcile cash collections against courier reports, handle fake notes, and chase missing remittances.
- **It is still essential.** The store that removes COD entirely will cut its return rate, but it will also lose a large share of customers who simply will not prepay.

The winning play in 2026 is not to drop COD. It is to keep COD while making prepaid options more attractive — a small discount for paying with JazzCash or Easypaisa, for example, nudges customers toward lower-risk payment without excluding anyone.

<Callout type="warning" title="COD return rates are the silent margin killer">
Industry reports and seller experience suggest return and refusal rates on COD orders can run anywhere from **15% to over 30%**, depending on the category and how well the product page sets expectations. On a PKR 5,000 order with a 30% return rate, you are financing delivery twice and often eating the return shipping. Do not set pricing and ad budgets on COD gross orders — model a realistic net after returns, or you will scale your way into a loss.
</Callout>

## Social Commerce and WhatsApp Selling

Social media is not a marketing channel in Pakistan — it is where commerce happens. Facebook and Instagram shops, TikTok storefronts and, above all, WhatsApp groups and broadcasts move a huge volume of orders every day, much of it completely outside traditional storefronts.

### The Seller-to-customer Conversation Economy

The most successful sellers in Pakistan run a personal-touch model: a customer sees a product on Instagram, messages the seller on WhatsApp, gets pictures and prices, and confirms the order in a short chat. No checkout form, no account creation, no saved card. This is comfortable for both sides, and it is why "WhatsApp Business" with a catalog is now a standard part of the toolkit for thousands of Pakistani sellers.

But that model has a ceiling. Everything lives in one phone. Orders, stock, customers and payments are mixed in with personal chats. When a seller grows past a few hundred orders a month, the manual WhatsApp workflow starts to leak money — missed messages, double-booked stock and no real data on where orders actually come from.

### Moving from WhatsApp Chaos to WhatsApp Structure

The stores that win in 2026 do not abandon WhatsApp — they put a storefront behind it:

- **Product pages deep-link into WhatsApp.** One tap opens a chat with the product name and price pre-loaded, so the seller never retypes details.
- **Catalogs sync with stock.** When a size or colour sells out on the website, it is not offered on WhatsApp anymore either.
- **AI agents handle the repetitive questions.** Sellers use an [**AI agent**](/ai-agents) to answer "price?", "available?", "delivery to Karachi?" and "COD possible?" instantly, 24/7, and hand off only the real conversations to a human.
- **Order IDs tie every chat to a record.** Even if payment happens at the door, the order is tracked from first message to delivered package.

The outcome is a hybrid: the trust and comfort of WhatsApp selling, with the inventory control and analytics of a real store. That combination is what separates sellers who stay at 50 orders a week from sellers who cross 500.

## Local Payment Wallets: the State of Play in 2026

JazzCash and Easypaisa are the two names every store owner should know, and together they dominate mobile-wallet usage in Pakistan. Between them — and a fast-growing crowd of neobanks and app-based wallets — millions of Pakistanis now hold a working mobile wallet, and that number has climbed steadily for years.

What has changed recently is how stores integrate them:

- **Wallet checkout is one tap.** Shoppers approve the payment inside an app they already trust, instead of typing card numbers into a browser.
- **QR payments are spreading.** Displaying a wallet QR code is now normal for in-person sellers and shows up in delivery workflows too.
- **Wallets work alongside COD, not just instead of it.** Many shoppers still default to cash at the door, but a small prepaid incentive converts a meaningful slice to wallet payments.

Payment gateways in Pakistan have also matured. A good gateway will connect your store to JazzCash, Easypaisa, the major bank cards and inter-bank transfers through one integration, with settlement in PKR. ## Logistics, Delivery and Returns

Delivery is where many Pakistani e-commerce ambitions come to die. Courier coverage, costs and behaviour vary sharply by city, and a store that treats the whole country as one delivery zone will bleed margin on every order.

Practical realities sellers should plan for in 2026:

- **Tier-one cities are reliable; tier-two is improving.** Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad and Rawalpindi have strong courier coverage with next-day or two-day delivery on most routes. Smaller cities and towns are better served than five years ago, but delivery times are longer and tracking can be patchy.
- **COD remittance cycles affect cash flow.** Courier companies collect cash on your behalf, but the money reaches your bank on their schedule. If you are running paid ads, you may need working capital to fund stock and ads before remittances arrive.
- **Returns are a real channel, not an edge case.** Every serious store needs a returns policy that is easy to find, a process for refused COD orders and damaged goods, and a way to put returned stock back into the catalogue quickly.
- **Free shipping thresholds shape basket size.** A well-placed "free delivery over PKR 2,500" threshold is one of the simplest levers to grow average order value — it works on Pakistani shoppers exactly as it does everywhere else.

For most sellers, the pragmatic approach is a multi-courier setup: use one or two reliable national couriers, negotiate volume rates, and test smaller regional providers where the national players are expensive or slow.

## AI in E-commerce: from Novelty to Baseline

Artificial intelligence is no longer a tech-glossary word in Pakistani e-commerce — it has become an operating tool that smaller stores can actually afford. The practical uses in 2026:

- **Customer support agents.** An [**AI agent**](/ai-agents) on your website or WhatsApp answers the same questions shoppers ask every day — stock, size, price, delivery time, COD availability — and escalates only when a human is truly needed.
- **Product copy and translations.** AI drafts Urdu and Roman-Urdu descriptions from your English source text in minutes, then a human reviews the result. Bilingual catalogues that used to take weeks now take days.
- **Catalogue photography and cleanup.** Background removal, image resizing and even simple AI-generated lifestyle shots bring second-rate product photos up to standard without a photographer.
- **Paid ads that self-optimise.** Meta and Google now bake AI into their ad systems. The store's job is to feed them clean conversion data, which is exactly why proper tracking matters more than ever.
- **Forecasting and stock decisions.** Simple models on your order history help you avoid overstocking slow movers and stockout-ing best sellers during seasonal peaks.

None of this replaces judgment. AI drafts, humans approve; AI predicts, humans decide. But the stores that adopt even two or three of these tools will run noticeably leaner than the ones still doing everything by hand.

## FAQs

### Is COD Still the Most Common Payment Method in Pakistan?

Yes. Reports consistently suggest cash on delivery still accounts for well over half of online orders in Pakistan, even in 2026. Wallets like JazzCash and Easypaisa are the fastest-growing segment, but most shoppers still prefer to pay at the door. Successful stores keep COD and use incentives — like a small prepaid discount — to shift customers toward lower-risk payments.

### Do I Need to Accept JazzCash and Easypaisa in My Store?

Effectively, yes. A large and growing share of Pakistani shoppers pay through these wallets, and many expect them at checkout. A good payment gateway connects your store to JazzCash, Easypaisa, bank cards and inter-bank transfers through one integration, so accepting them is not an either/or decision.

### How Do I Reduce COD Returns?

Start with expectations. Accurate photos, honest size guides, real delivery-time estimates and clear product descriptions cut a meaningful share of refusals before the order is even placed. Then add friction where it helps: a small prepaid discount shifts some buyers to wallets, and confirming orders by WhatsApp before dispatch reduces cold-door refusals. Finally, track refusal rates by product and city so you can fix the worst offenders rather than subsidising them.

### Should I Sell on Marketplaces or Build My Own Store?

Run both, with clear roles. Marketplaces like Daraz give you reach and volume but take a cut and keep the customer relationship. Your own store is where margin, data and repeat business live. Use the marketplace to grow, then move buyers onto your own storefront with better pricing, bundles and WhatsApp retention.

### Can AI Really Help a Small Store in Pakistan?

Yes, in very practical ways. AI agents answer routine customer questions on WhatsApp day and night, AI drafts Urdu and Roman-Urdu product descriptions in minutes, and the ad platforms now use AI to optimise campaigns automatically — provided you feed them clean conversion data. The work AI cannot do is decide; a human still owns strategy, stock and pricing.
